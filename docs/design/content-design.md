# Content Design

## Purpose

Content should help people understand Fayette Habitat’s local work, decide what to do, and trust what they are reading. This guide sets shared language, structure, evidence, privacy, and maintenance expectations for public and administrative content. It does not replace participant consent, legal review, program expertise, or the human verification backlog.

## Voice

Fayette Habitat’s voice should be:

- **Neighborly:** grounded in Fayette County, direct, and welcoming without forced folksiness.
- **Respectful:** people retain agency and complexity; their circumstances are not promotional devices.
- **Specific:** name the work, place, period, source, and next step when verified.
- **Capable:** explain operations and stewardship clearly without institutional boasting.
- **Hopeful and honest:** show progress and possibility without hiding constraints or promising outcomes the organization cannot guarantee.
- **Actionable:** tell people what they can do, what will happen next, and which system or team will respond.

Tone may become more concise for News and operational guidance, more reflective for Stories, more procedural for Programs and forms/handoffs, and more evidentiary for impact/grant material. The underlying voice should remain consistent.

## Language rules

### Write for people, not departments

- Lead with the audience’s question and use familiar terms.
- Define Habitat-specific, grant, construction, and CRM language when it cannot be avoided.
- Use concrete verbs: “register to volunteer,” “give to this project,” “check ReStore hours,” “read the project update.”
- Avoid vague link and button labels such as “Learn more,” “Click here,” or repeated “Submit” when a specific action fits.
- Distinguish money donations, ReStore item donations, volunteer applications, event registrations, newsletter signup, and merchandise purchases.

### Describe people with dignity

- Use the language an identified person approves for themself.
- Center partnership, goals, work, and agency rather than pity, rescue, or deservingness.
- Do not disclose applicant status, eligibility, household composition, financial circumstances, disability, exact address, or services received unless deliberately approved for the specific public use.
- Do not use “before and after” framing that reduces a person to a condition or project outcome.
- Do not imply that one participant represents every homeowner, applicant, volunteer, or community member.

### Make certainty visible

- State verified facts plainly.
- Qualify estimates, goals, projections, reporting periods, and methodological limits.
- If content is unverified, route it to `docs/product/content-verification.md`; do not fill the space with plausible copy.
- **Unknown content is not an unknown requirement.** The product can proceed while affected claims remain unpublished.

## Canonical terminology

| Term | Use | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Story | Internal name for enduring editorial narrative; public label remains open | Treating every post or update as a Story |
| News | Timely, concise announcement or operational update | Calling evergreen narrative News; generic “blog post” |
| Project | A structured, bounded unit of Habitat work | Using Story pages as the only project record |
| Program | A maintained, continuing service or line of work | Using Program and Project interchangeably |
| Campaign | Fayette Habitat’s narrative, goal, updates, related work, and call to support | Assuming it means a DonorView fund/appeal/destination |
| Donation destination | Approved provider-neutral hosted page, embed, or later flow where a gift is completed | Implying Campaign presentation owns the gift record |
| Featured | A deliberate content placement for a context and period | Synonyms such as latest, urgent, or permanently important |
| Expired News | No longer current under an editorial rule but retained according to archive policy | Deleted, inaccurate, or necessarily withdrawn |
| Withdrawn | Removed from normal public availability by a deliberate editorial action | Expired or archived |
| Public Grant Impact | Approved acknowledgment and evidence selected for public use | A view of the private grant record |
| Applicant / participant / homeowner | Use only when accurate, necessary, and approved for context | “Recipient” or labels that imply a private case relationship |
| ReStore | Use the verified local public name and capitalization | Treating ReStore as unrelated retail or a footer utility |

## Content-type guidance

### Stories

Promise: an enduring, meaningful narrative that adds context beyond an announcement.

A Story should ordinarily establish:

- a clear audience-focused title and summary/deck;
- why the story matters now and over time;
- a coherent narrative with descriptive headings;
- approved authorship/byline and publication/update context;
- consented, credited, accessible media where useful;
- connections to verified Projects, Programs, People, Campaigns, Events, Grants, Partners, ReStore, or Artists where relevant;
- contextual evidence without turning the piece into a metric collage; and
- a useful next step, or an intentional conclusion when no action is appropriate.

Pull quotes must repeat text already available in the reading order or be introduced as new quoted content with appropriate attribution. Galleries need meaningful sequence, alt text or decorative treatment, and captions/credits where context or rights require them.

### News

Promise: a timely, authoritative update that lets readers understand what changed and whether action is needed.

A News item should ordinarily include:

- a direct title that communicates the development;
- a short summary;
- publication date and, when needed, update/effective/expiration context;
- the essential who/what/when/where/why without burying urgent details;
- a verified action, contact, registration, or source link when relevant;
- optional related Project, Program, Campaign, Event, Grant, Partner, and Media; and
- a clear correction, expiration, archive, cancellation, or withdrawal treatment when circumstances change.

Do not imply that Featured News is latest, urgent, or permanent. Do not use a temporary News item as the only maintained source for canonical Program rules, ReStore hours, or Event details.

### Programs

A Program explanation should answer:

- what the Program does and does not do;
- who it is for and the verified geographic scope;
- current eligibility/participation context without promising acceptance;
- what someone should prepare or expect, without soliciting sensitive data through unsafe channels;
- the official next step and responsible contact/system;
- when the information was reviewed; and
- related Projects, Stories, News, or impact evidence that improve understanding.

### Projects

Project content should distinguish verified status, work scope, dates/phase, Program, broad location where safe, Partners/Grants, related communications, and impact. Never infer or expose a participant’s private case, exact home address, eligibility, household details, or documents from the Project record.

### Campaigns and giving

A Campaign should explain the need, goal and scope, related Project/Program, what support enables, progress methodology/as-of time where shown, approved donation destination, updates, and final impact reporting.

Before an external giving handoff, state the purpose and action clearly. Do not claim a gift is tax-deductible, restricted, matched, fee-free, receipted, refundable, or allocated in a particular way without verification. The Campaign owns the public narrative; the approved provider owns donor/gift records.

### Events

Lead with the current edition’s date, time zone, venue/format, status, accessibility/accommodation route, price or free status, registration/ticket owner, capacity/deadline where relevant, and cancellation/weather information. Historical event content must look historical, not accidentally upcoming.

### ReStore

Maintain one canonical source for ordinary location, hours, contact, accessibility, accepted-item/shopping guidance, and mission relationship. Use News for temporary exceptions such as holiday hours or closures, linked back to canonical information. Add a reviewed-at date and named internal owner.

### Impact and public grant content

Every material measure should identify:

- what is counted and what is excluded;
- geography and reporting period;
- source and calculation/method context;
- whether it is a goal, estimate, output, outcome, point-in-time value, or cumulative value;
- as-of/update date and accountable owner; and
- related Program, Project, Campaign, or Grant context.

Public Grant Impact may use only approved acknowledgment and evidence. Never summarize private applications, agreements, budgets, contacts, notes, or reporting drafts merely because they are related.

### Leadership and governance

Use verified titles, terms, names, bios, portraits/credits, and as-of context. Separate public People profiles from administrator accounts. Avoid publishing personal contact details or internal responsibilities by accident.

### Shop and Artists

Product copy should state verified material/fit/variant information, price, availability, artist credit/relationship, fulfillment, return/exchange, and pickup/shipping details. Clearly distinguish a merchandise purchase from a charitable contribution.

## Calls to action and external handoffs

A call to action should make its result predictable.

Preferred pattern:

1. specific action label;
2. one sentence of essential expectation when the action is consequential or external;
3. destination/provider cue where it helps trust;
4. privacy, eligibility, deadline, cost, or accessibility information needed before action; and
5. recovery/contact path for a broken or unavailable destination.

Examples of intent (not approved production copy):

- Register to volunteer
- Give to this campaign
- View current ReStore hours
- Buy Casino Night tickets
- Sign up for News and Stories
- Request program information

Do not label an external link “Apply now” if it opens an informational page, or “Donate” if it opens a merchandise checkout.

## Dates, status, and corrections

- Use absolute dates for public operational information; do not rely on “today,” “tomorrow,” or “this weekend” without the date nearby.
- Include the time zone when ambiguity is plausible.
- Separate publication date from event/effective date.
- Label postponement, cancellation, expiration, archive, or withdrawal directly.
- When a material published fact changes, follow the approved correction policy; do not silently edit history when readers need to know the correction.
- Avoid “last updated” unless the update has meaning and an accountable owner; prefer “Hours reviewed August 2026” or equivalent type-specific context when verified.

## Links and labels

- Link text should identify destination or action out of context.
- Avoid raw URLs in body copy unless the URL itself is meaningful information.
- Indicate file type/size when a download may surprise users.
- Mark external destinations consistently without stuffing every label with “opens in a new window.”
- Do not force new windows; if one is necessary, disclose behavior accessibly.
- Check external links before publication and on an owned cadence.
- Never place secrets, preview tokens, private document paths, or sensitive identifiers in content links.

## Media content

Every media asset needs, as applicable:

- a rights/provenance record and required credit;
- participant consent and restrictions;
- alt text based on purpose/context, or explicit decorative status;
- caption for information not conveyed in surrounding prose;
- transcript/captions/audio description for time-based media as needed;
- content warning where appropriate and approved;
- focal point/crop guidance that does not remove meaningful context; and
- public/private classification and retention/review information.

Alt text should communicate the image’s purpose in context, not duplicate the caption or infer sensitive characteristics. Do not start automatically with “Image of.” For linked images, the accessible name should communicate the link action or destination.

## Search and SEO content

- Write unique, accurate titles and descriptions for people first.
- Use one descriptive page title and a logical heading structure.
- Keep canonical records authoritative; avoid near-duplicate Story, News, Project, and Event copy.
- Preserve valuable legacy traffic through redirect decisions, not by copying Wix titles or URL structure into the new architecture.
- Use structured data only when visible content supports it and the type is accurate.
- Archived News and past Events must not masquerade as current in search snippets.

## Administrative microcopy

Administrative content should explain consequence and recovery:

- Name actions precisely: Submit for review, Approve this revision, Schedule publication, Withdraw from public view.
- Show which revision, date/time, placement, and destination an action affects.
- Explain why publication is blocked and how to resolve it.
- Distinguish validation, warning, permission denial, provider failure, and system failure.
- For destructive or difficult-to-reverse actions, state the exact target and retained history before confirmation.
- Do not expose capabilities, private review notes, or content a staff member lacks authority to see merely to explain a denial.

## Content lifecycle and ownership

Every maintained public record should have:

- an accountable content owner;
- publication/review authority;
- source/evidence for consequential facts;
- effective, event, publication, expiration, or reviewed-at dates appropriate to type;
- a correction and archive/retirement path;
- external-link maintainer where applicable; and
- privacy, rights, consent, and accessibility readiness.

The Communications Dashboard may surface freshness and quality risks, but a dashboard does not replace named ownership.

## Editorial readiness checklist

Before publication, confirm:

- the content fulfills the selected type’s promise;
- title, summary, headings, and next action are clear;
- dates, status, source, and time zone are accurate;
- claims are verified and appropriately qualified;
- participant privacy and consent are documented;
- Media has rights, credit, alt text/decorative treatment, captions/transcripts as needed;
- links and external handoffs are accurate, accessible, and owned;
- related records are meaningful and public-safe;
- SEO/share metadata is truthful and not sensational;
- the exact revision has the required approval; and
- expiration, archive, correction, and placement behavior is understood.

Legacy content must pass this checklist and receive an explicit migration disposition. Its existence on Wix is not approval.
